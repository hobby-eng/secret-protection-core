### Related findings

- The Linux worker channel: AUD-021-SEC001 (pipes reopened through /proc), then AUD-022-SEC001 (the start-up window), AUD-023-SEC001 with UL-2 (trust in the report after that window), and AUD-024-SEC002 (user-owned namespaces). ebfb348 closes the last three by starting no worker where another process of the user may attach.
- Windows thread access: AUD-024-SEC005 (the token reached through the process list) and AUD-024-SEC006 (threads started before the census).
- The launch deadline: AUD-022-API001, AUD-023-API001 and the test gap AUD-024-BLD004.
- The terminal restore: AUD-022-FUN001, AUD-023-FUN001 and AUD-023-DOC002.
- The announced frame length: AUD-023-API002 (lengths past what a `Secret` can hold) and AUD-024-SEC001 (any length up to `most`). Different causes; ebfb348 fixed the first in code and the second in documentation.

### Disagreements and open questions

- AUD-024-SEC001 (P-01) and AUD-024-ARC001 (P-07) are closed by documentation or a comment; the behaviour they describe is unchanged.
- AUD-024-SEC005 to AUD-024-SEC007 and AUD-024-DOC004 (AW-1 to AW-4) rest on reasoning from vendor documentation. No native run reproduced the attacks: the CI tests show the narrower settings in force. AW-1 keeps the reader's High.
- AUD-023-BLD001 names Windows; P-02 also found WebAssembly. One entry covers both.
